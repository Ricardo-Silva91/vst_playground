Lo-Fi Tape
Ran it through a worn-out Walkman.

Lizard Tape is a cassette in a plugin: asymmetric tape saturation, real wow and flutter, a darkened top end and hiss that follows the music. The pitch wobble comes from a modulated delay with cubic interpolation, not a chorus, so chords bend the way a stretched tape does. You can try it in your browser below before you download it.

macOS Windows ▶ Try it live
Signal Chain
Input› Drive · saturation› Wow & flutter› Tone roll-off› Hiss› Mix · Output

Each channel runs the same chain. Drive pushes the signal into a tanh curve that Bias tilts off-centre, then gain makeup brings the level back down. The result goes through a 12 ms delay line whose read point is swept by a slow wow LFO, a fast flutter LFO and a little random drift. A one-pole low-pass rolls off the top, and hiss is added after it, a bit louder while the music is playing. Every knob is smoothed over about 20 ms, so automation doesn't click. The wet path always sits 12 ms behind the dry signal, and the plugin doesn't report that delay to the host.

Mix at 0 is bit-identical dry

With Mix at 0 and Output at 0 dB, the output matches the input sample for sample, even with every other knob at an extreme. Bypass then changes nothing.

Max drive stays bounded

Drive 24 dB and Bias 1 on the sweep. The tanh curve caps the peaks, and the makeup gain pulls loud peaks down by about 13 dB, so heavy drive sounds denser, not louder.

Silence in, only hiss out

On silence the only output is a low hiss bed, about −49 dB RMS at this setting. Under a loud signal it gets about 4 dB louder.

Wow and flutter bend the pitch

A steady 1 kHz tone wavers audibly. The pitch plot shows the slow wow swing with the fast flutter ripple on top of it.

Partial Mix combs

At 50 % Mix the dry signal and the wet signal, 12 ms later, notch out every 83 Hz. On the sweep you hear a regular whoosh. With wow on, the notches move and the whole thing flanges.

The demo runs a JavaScript port of lizard_tape/Source/DSP/TapeProcessor.h, plus the processor's per-block parameter wiring, in an AudioWorklet. It is not the compiled plugin. The port includes the core's std::mt19937 noise source and rounds to 32-bit float the same way the C++ does. It was checked against a native build of the same core: 0 mismatches over 1,536,000 samples across 5 settings at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, including parameter changes partway through. That check used correctly rounded tanhf and powf. The library versions on each platform round the last bit differently, so the compiled plugin can differ from this demo by about one part in ten million. As in the plugin, the parameters set new smoothing targets once per 128-sample block. Source: lizard_tape on GitHub.

Saturation
Drive

How hard the signal hits the tanh curve. Gain makeup brings the level back down as you turn it up, so high Drive gets denser and quieter, not louder.

Range: 0 → 24 dB    Default: 4 dB
Sat Bias

Moves the curve's operating point off-centre, so the positive and negative halves clip differently. That adds even harmonics (warmth) on top of the odd ones (grit).

Range: 0 → 1    Default: 0.15
Wow & Flutter
Wow Depth · Rate

The slow pitch drift of an uneven capstan. A sine at Rate swings the delay by half of Depth either way, plus a much smaller random drift so no two cycles are identical.

Depth: 0 → 10 ms, default 3    Rate: 0.05 → 4 Hz, default 0.7
Flutter Depth · Rate

The fast shimmer of a worn pinch roller. At high rates it sounds less like pitch and more like a grainy roughness.

Depth: 0 → 3 ms, default 0.6    Rate: 2 → 16 Hz, default 8
Tone & Hiss
Tone

A one-pole low-pass that models tape losing the top end. It's gentle, 6 dB per octave, so even low settings sound dull rather than muffled.

Range: 800 → 18,000 Hz    Default: 6,500 Hz
Hiss

Mostly white noise with a little low-end weight, added after the Tone filter. It's up to 4 dB louder while the music plays than in the gaps, like real tape. At 1 it sits about 45 dB below full scale on silence.

Range: 0 → 1    Default: 0.06
Mix & Output
Mix

Dry/wet blend. 0 passes the input through bit for bit. In between, the 12 ms wet delay combs against the dry signal, so use 100 % unless you want that flanged sound.

Range: 0 → 1    Default: 1
Output

Final trim, applied after the mix.

Range: −24 → +12 dB    Default: 0 dB
Starting Points
Worn Walkman

Noticeable wow, a bit of flutter, a 4.5 kHz top end and audible hiss. The tape you left in the car all summer.

Warm Deck

10 dB of drive with more bias, almost no wobble, and an open top end. Good tape glue for a bus.

Seasick

The full 10 ms of slow wow. Chords drift in and out of tune. Use it in small doses.

Dictaphone

Hard drive, fast flutter, a 2.2 kHz roll-off and lots of hiss. A voice memo from 1989.

Clean

Mix at 0, so the output is bit-identical to the input. Bring Mix up to hear the tape.

These are the starting points in the live demo above. They are suggestions for the plugin's knobs, not factory presets inside the plugin.

Use Cases
Lo-fi keys and pads

Sustained chords show off wow best. A few milliseconds of slow wow and a darker Tone make a clean Rhodes or pad sound sampled off an old cassette.

Wow 3–5 ms @ 0.5–0.9 Hz · Flutter 0.5 ms · Tone 4–6 kHz · Hiss 0.1
Drum bus saturation

Turn the wobble off and use only Drive and Bias for rounded, glued transients. Keep Mix at 100 %, because a partial mix combs.

Drive 8–12 dB · Bias 0.3–0.5 · Wow & Flutter 0 · Tone 12–18 kHz · Hiss 0
Deliberate flange

The 12 ms wet delay against the dry signal at a partial Mix is a flanger. Add a little wow and the notches sweep.

Mix 40–60 % · Wow 1–2 ms @ 0.2 Hz · Flutter 0
Voice-memo vocals

On a vocal or a spoken sample, use low Tone, heavy Drive and fast flutter for a handheld recorder sound.

Drive 14 dB · Flutter 2 ms @ 12 Hz · Tone 2–3 kHz · Hiss 0.4–0.6
Tips and Workflow
▶

Keep Mix at 100 % on parallel buses. The wet path is 12 ms late, and the plugin doesn't report that delay to the host, so blending it with a dry copy, in the plugin or on another track, combs. Nudge the dry track 12 ms later if you need a clean blend.

▶

Turn Output up after heavy Drive. The makeup gain overcorrects on loud material at high Drive (about −13 dB at 24 dB of drive). Use Output to level-match before you judge the sound with Bypass.

▶

Wow depth scales with rate. The pitch swing is depth × rate, so 3 ms at 4 Hz bends much further than 3 ms at 0.5 Hz. The demo's “Wow pitch swing” readout shows the result in cents.

▶

Both channels hiss alike. Every channel's noise starts from the same seed, so the hiss and the drift are identical left and right. The hiss sits in the centre, and stereo material wobbles together.
